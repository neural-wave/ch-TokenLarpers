# Neural wave Hackathon

## Guidelines for Project Submission

To ensure a correct validation for your hackathon project, please follow these steps:

### Step 1: Code Submission
1. **Organize Your Code**:  
   - Create a well-structured repository with clear folder naming (e.g., `src/`, `data/`, `notebooks/`).  
   - Include a `README.md` with an overview, instructions for running the code, and dependencies.
   - Include a 3-page report. The jury will look at it!
   - Pepare a 2-min summary video. The judges will look at it and it will be part of the evaluation.
   - In case of any doubts, make sure to ask in advance (in person or on Discord).
   - To be sure, we advise to test your submission in a clean environment.
2. **Upload the repo to the Neural Wave organization**:  
   - Make sure to list dependencies in `requirements.txt` or `environment.yaml`. The environment must be created with a simple `pip install`.
   - Read carefully the submission requirement of your specific challenge. They might require outputs in a specific format or a specific API.
      - Failing to comply to project-specific requirements, will most definitely invalidate your submission!

### Step 2: Report Creation
1. **Report Content**:  
   - Write a report max 3 pages:
     - **Project Title**: Brief and descriptive.    
     - **Approach**: Methodology, algorithms, architecture and frameworks used and the motivation for selecting them.
     - **Results**: Key findings, metrics, or visualizations.  
     - **Challenges**: Difficulties encountered and solutions. List ideas that ended up not working or did not perform well.
     - **AI Agents/LLM usage:** Be transparent about the usage of external agentic tools and LLM-based coding assistants.
   
2. **Format and Publishing**:  
   - Keep the report concise and well-formatted.  
   - Include images, tables, or links as necessary.
   - Nobody wants to read AI slop, be human! 

### Step 3: Video Explanation
1. **Record a Video**:  
   - Create a short video max 2 minutes that explains:  
     - Your project goal and relevance.  
     - Key results and their interpretation.  
   - Use visuals like charts, diagrams, or code snippets for better understanding.
   - A recorded demo of the model, if applicable, is very appreciated.
   - The video is watched first and should represent your effort as much as possible. The report is read in a second stage.
2. **Upload the Video**:  
   - Be ready to provide us with the video; prepare a Swisstransfer. (https://www.swisstransfer.com) link with the video file.

### Step 4: Final Submission and Publishing
1. **Check All Files**:  
   - Ensure your code, report, and video are properly formatted and included.  
   - Review for completeness and accuracy. 
   - Ensure to give credit to any external sources used in your project. No plagiarism is allowed.
   - Make sure the model entry points work out of the box. We will not debug. 
2. **Other remarks**:
   - Only one submission per team
   - You will find a repo called `NW_26_<Company Name>_<Team Name>`, one team member will be added as collaborator (you can invite each other afterwards). The readme must include all member's full name, and possibly their role.
   - Make sure that everything is correctly published by 11am on Sunday at the latest.

### Step 5: Prepare a short 5 minute presentation while the projects are being judges
   - One last task before some deserved rest; while the judges check the submissions, prepare a 5 minute presentation of your solution. You will present it in case you win!
   - Of course, it's possible to prepare it in advance, should you have extra time.
